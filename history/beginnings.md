# Beginnings of Trust & Safety

The concept of **Trust & Safety (T&S)** began in the early days of the internet, when platforms first realized that online communities needed protection, rules, and accountability to grow safely.

## 🌍 Early Internet Challenges (1990s)
- The rise of chat rooms, forums, and early social networks brought millions of people online.
- Platforms allowed users to post freely, but there were **no systems to prevent abuse or misinformation**.
- Problems like harassment, scams, and inappropriate content began to appear.

## 🛡️ First Safety Measures (Late 1990s–2000s)
- Platforms introduced **basic community rules** and started hiring small teams of moderators.
- Email and forum platforms began using **spam filters** and **keyword-based blocking**.
- Reporting features and user bans became early forms of enforcement.

## 🚀 Growth of the Profession (2000s–2010s)
- Large platforms like **YouTube (2005)**, **Facebook (2004)**, and **Twitter (2006)** scaled quickly, creating the need for **dedicated Trust & Safety teams**.
- Content moderation expanded beyond spam — covering areas like **child safety, hate speech, and misinformation**.
- Companies built **policy enforcement playbooks**, **review tools**, and **training programs** for moderators.

## 🤖 Modern Era & Automation (2010s–Now)
- The explosion of user-generated content pushed platforms to use **machine learning** and **AI-assisted moderation**.
- Specialized tools like **automated flagging**, **hash-matching for harmful content**, and **proactive detection systems** became standard.
- New roles emerged: **Policy Managers, Integrity Analysts, and T&S Program Managers**.

## 🧭 Key Takeaways
- Trust & Safety evolved because **the internet needs guardrails** to protect users and build trust.
- The field combines **policy, technology, and operations**.
- What began as simple community rules is now a **global profession** shaping how billions of people interact online.
